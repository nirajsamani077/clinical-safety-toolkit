## AI scribe

An AI scribe transcribes the consultation and drafts the notes. Same method: start from the supplier's hazard log and add your own. For example:

| Cause | Effect | Hazard | Controls and mitigations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The AI mishears, or "fills in" plausible detail (it "hallucinates"). Because the notes are usually right, the clinician skims instead of reading (automation bias) | The note or letter contains something that wasn't said, and it's saved unnoticed | A patient is treated on the basis of a wrong record | Clinician reads every note before saving; monthly sample audit of notes; training covers automation bias and why checking matters |
| The plan or safety-netting is said after the recording stops | The note is missing the plan | A patient's follow-up or safety-netting is missed | Clinician checks the plan and safety-netting are in the note before saving |
| Back-to-back patients: the clinician doesn't start a new session, or copies the note into EMIS after the next patient's record is already open | One patient's note is saved in another patient's record | Two patients' records are wrong, and the next clinician acts on someone else's history or plan | A new session for every patient; check the name on the scribe matches the open record before pasting; use the direct EMIS link where there is one |

Asking the patient's permission matters too, but it's a data protection and confidentiality question rather than a clinical safety hazard. See the section on [telling patients](/information-governance/telling-patients).

NHS England expects practices using AI scribes to have a CSO and complete DCB0160, as well as a DPIA from the information governance side.<sup><a href="#ref-1">1</a></sup>

## A calculator built into your clinical system

Calculators inside the clinical system or prescribing software, such as QRISK or creatinine clearance, feel like part of the furniture, but they're health IT too. The [QRISK2 error](/clinical-safety/understand#why-it-matters) went unnoticed for years. Two hazards a practice might log:

| Cause | Effect | Hazard | Controls and mitigations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The creatinine clearance calculation uses an out-of-date weight, or eGFR is used in its place | Kidney function is overestimated | A patient on a DOAC (such as apixaban) is prescribed a dose too high for their kidney function | Record a current weight before calculating; use creatinine clearance, not eGFR, for DOAC doses, as the MHRA advises<sup><a href="#ref-2">2</a></sup>; pharmacist check at annual DOAC review |
| A system update changes how a built-in risk calculator reads the record | Risk scores are wrong, with no warning on screen | A patient isn't offered treatment they need, such as a statin, or is offered one they don't | After each system update, check a few scores against the official online calculator; ask the supplier to flag calculator changes in release notes |
