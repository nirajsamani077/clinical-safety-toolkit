## Stage 1: Prepare

**Add it to your [technology register](/templates/technology-register)**, your list of every digital tool you use.<sup><a href="#ref-1">1</a></sup>

**Agree who your CSO is.** See [help](/clinical-safety/help).

**Ask the supplier:**<sup><a href="#ref-2">2</a></sup>

<ul class="askq">
  <li>Can you send your clinical safety case report and hazard log?</li>
  <li>How do you inform us about changes to the product?</li>
  <li>Is it a <a href="/explainers/medical-devices">medical device</a>? What class?</li>
  <li>How do we report a clinical safety problem to you?</li>
</ul>

If your ICB or PCN already has a DCB0160 hazard log for the same tool, start from it, but remember your documents need to be specific to how you use the tool locally.

## Stage 2: Assess

**Hold a [hazard workshop](/templates/hazard-workshop-pack).** A meeting with the people who'll use the tool, reception included. Walk through the workflow and ask at each step what could go wrong.

A simple way to do this is the **Structured What-If Technique** (SWIFT), which NHS England teaches on its clinical safety course. At each step of the workflow, ask "what if...?": what if the wrong information is entered, the system is down, someone leaves without saving, or two records are open at once? Write each answer down as cause, effect, hazard and harm.

**Write the [hazard log](/templates/hazard-log).** Start from the supplier's, then add hazards from your own set-up. Each row reads left to right:

## Stage 3: Approve

**Write the [clinical safety case report](/templates/safety-case-report).** Two to six pages is plenty; it doesn't have to be a long essay:

<ol class="outline" data-title="Clinical safety case report">
  <li>What the tool is and how you'll use it</li>
  <li>How you assessed it</li>
  <li>The main hazards and controls</li>
  <li>The risk that remains, and why it's acceptable</li>
  <li>When you'll review it</li>
</ol>

The CSO signs it, and the partners review the residual risk before agreeing to go live with the tool.

## Stage 5: Monitor and review

Keep an incident log and review the hazard log when anything changes. It isn't a one-off: it carries on as the product and your practice's ways of working change. See [after go-live](/clinical-safety/after-go-live).
