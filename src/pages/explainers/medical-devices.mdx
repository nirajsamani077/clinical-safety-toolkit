## What makes software a medical device

It depends on its intended purpose: what the manufacturer says the software is for. Software that helps diagnose, treat or prevent illness is more likely to be a device. Software that stores, displays or transcribes information usually isn't.

If it is a device, it must be registered with the MHRA before it's placed on the market in Great Britain.<sup><a href="#ref-1">1</a></sup> It should carry a UKCA or CE mark.

## The classes

Devices are put into one of four classes by risk. The higher the class, the more checking is needed before the product can be sold.<sup><a href="#ref-2">2</a></sup>

<ClassLadder />

How the examples fit:

- **Risk levels, and who checks each class,** come from the MHRA.<sup><a href="#ref-2">2</a></sup>
- **The everyday examples** come from the NHS Innovation Service guide to device regulation.<sup><a href="#ref-3">3</a></sup>
- **The software examples for Class I to IIb** follow the MHRA's guidance on software and apps. Software that allows a direct diagnosis is at least Class IIa, and software that monitors vital functions where a change could be immediately dangerous is Class IIb.<sup><a href="#ref-4">4</a></sup>
- **Under EU rules,** which many suppliers also follow for their CE mark, software tends to sit in higher classes than in Great Britain. Software informing decisions that could cause serious harm is IIb, and death or irreversible harm is III.<sup><a href="#ref-3">3</a></sup>

<div class="split-note">
<strong>The UK rules are changing.</strong> The MHRA published draft new regulations in May 2026.<sup><a href="#ref-5">5</a></sup> The classification rules are being rewritten, so a product's class may change. Ask suppliers how they're preparing.
</div>

## What to do

<div class="steps-v">
  <div><b>1</b><p><strong>Ask the supplier</strong> whether the product is a medical device, what its class is, and what its intended purpose is.</p></div>
  <div><b>2</b><p><strong>If it is a device,</strong> check it's registered on the MHRA's <a href="https://pard.mhra.gov.uk/">public register of devices</a>, and report problems through the <a href="https://yellowcard.mhra.gov.uk/">Yellow Card scheme</a>.</p></div>
  <div><b>3</b><p><strong>Watch for new features.</strong> A scribe that starts suggesting diagnoses may have changed category. Ask before switching new features on.</p></div>
  <div><b>4</b><p><strong>Either way,</strong> you still need your own <a href="/clinical-safety/process">clinical safety assessment</a> and <a href="/information-governance/dpia">DPIA</a>. Not being a device doesn't mean low risk.</p></div>
</div>
