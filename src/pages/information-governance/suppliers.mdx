## Three ways to protect data

At each step in a data flow, ask how the data is protected, and whether the supplier needs to know who the patient is.

<div class="optcards">
  <div class="optcard" style="--c:#0b4a6e"><span class="ib"><Icon name="lock" /></span><strong>Encryption</strong><p>Scrambles data so only someone with the key can read it. Ask for it both while data moves between systems and where it's stored.<sup><a href="#ref-2">2</a></sup></p><small>Still personal data</small></div>
  <div class="optcard" style="--c:#920570"><span class="ib"><Icon name="key" /></span><strong>Pseudonymisation</strong><p>Swaps names and NHS numbers for a code. Whoever holds the key can link it back to the patient, for example an AI scribe working on a transcript.<sup><a href="#ref-3">3</a></sup></p><small>Still personal data</small></div>
  <div class="optcard" style="--c:#3b7a14"><span class="ib"><Icon name="eye" /></span><strong>Anonymisation</strong><p>Removes anything that could identify the patient, so it can't be linked back. This is the only form a supplier should use to improve its product.<sup><a href="#ref-3">3</a></sup></p><small>No longer personal data</small></div>
</div>

## The data processing agreement (DPA)

The law requires a written contract.<sup><a href="#ref-1">1</a></sup> In plain English, the supplier promises to:

<ul class="ticks">
  <li><strong>Follow your instructions</strong><span>Only use the data the way you've agreed.</span></li>
  <li><strong>Keep it secure</strong><span>Appropriate technical and organisational security.</span></li>
  <li><strong>Ask before subcontracting</strong><span>Only use other companies with your permission, on the same terms.</span></li>
  <li><strong>Tell you about breaches</strong><span>Promptly, so you can meet your own deadlines.</span></li>
  <li><strong>Delete or return it</strong><span>When the contract ends.</span></li>
</ul>

Ask your DPO to read the supplier's standard agreement before you sign.

## Questions to ask before you sign

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Where is the data stored and processed? Is any of it outside the UK?</li>
  <li>Do you use our data to train or improve your product, including AI models?</li>
  <li>Have you completed the Data Security and Protection Toolkit, and do you hold <a href="/explainers/cyber-security">Cyber Essentials Plus</a>?</li>
  <li>Can you provide a draft DPIA and your data processing agreement?</li>
</ul>

## Warning signs

<div class="tiles tight">
  <div class="tile flag"><span class="ib"><Icon name="alert" /></span><strong>No agreement offered</strong><span>Or one that lets the supplier use data "for any purpose".</span></div>
  <div class="tile flag"><span class="ib"><Icon name="refresh" /></span><strong>"To improve our services"</strong><span>Data reuse with no detail about what that means.</span></div>
  <div class="tile flag"><span class="ib"><Icon name="phone" /></span><strong>Free or consumer versions</strong><span>A personal or free version being used for patient work.</span></div>

## When the ICB or NHS England buys the tool

Usually the practice is still the data controller for its own patients. Ask for the central agreement and DPIA, check they cover how you use the tool, and add your own where needed.
