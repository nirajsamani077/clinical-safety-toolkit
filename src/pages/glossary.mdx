| Term | Plain English |
| --- | --- |
| **AI policy** | A short practice document saying which AI tools staff can use, what for, and how a new one gets approved |
| **AI scribe** (ambient voice technology, AVT) | A tool that listens to a consultation and drafts the notes |
| **AI tool register** | The practice's list of AI tools, with each one's status and what it may be used for |
| **Automation bias** | Trusting a tool's output without checking it, because it's usually right |
| **Bow-tie** | A picture of one hazard in the middle, its causes on the left and the harms it could lead to on the right. Controls sit on the cause side; mitigations sit on the harm side |
| **Caldicott Guardian** | The person responsible for protecting patient confidentiality |
| **Caldicott Principles** | Eight principles from the National Data Guardian for using confidential information well |
| **Clinical risk management file** | The folder holding all the safety documents for your tools |
| **Clinical safety case report (CSCR)** | A short report arguing that a tool is safe to use here, signed by the CSO |
| **Clinical Safety Officer (CSO)** | A registered clinician trained in clinical risk management who signs off safety work |
| **Controls** | The things you do to reduce a risk |
| **CRMP** (Clinical Risk Management Plan) | How you'll assess one particular tool |
| **CRMS** (Clinical Risk Management System) | How your practice does clinical safety in general |
| **Cyber Essentials** | A government-backed certificate showing five basic cyber security protections are in place. Plus adds hands-on testing. [Explainer](/explainers/cyber-security) |
| **Data controller** | The organisation that decides how and why personal information is used. For its patients, the practice |
| **Data processing agreement** | The contract setting out what a supplier may do with data on your behalf |
| **Data processor** | An organisation that handles personal information on someone else’s instructions, such as a supplier |
| **DCB0129** | The safety standard for companies that make health software |
| **DCB0160** | The safety standard for organisations that use health software, including GP practices |
| **Digital lead** | The named person responsible for digital and AI tools in the practice. CQC expects one where AI is used |
| **DPIA** (Data Protection Impact Assessment) | The data protection check for a new way of using patient data |
| **DPO** (Data Protection Officer) | The person who advises on data protection law |
| **DSPT** (Data Security and Protection Toolkit) | The yearly data security self-assessment every practice completes. [Explainer](/explainers/dspt) |
| **DTAC** (Digital Technology Assessment Criteria) | NHS England's baseline checklist for digital products. [Explainer](/explainers/dtac) |
| **Cause** | Something that could make a hazard happen, usually a problem with the technology or how it’s used. There is often more than one |
| **Effect** | What goes wrong with the information because of a cause, before anyone is at risk. For example, an urgent request sitting in the routine queue |
| **Hallucination** | When an AI tool produces something fluent and confident that is wrong |
| **Harm** | What could happen to the patient: injury, illness, delayed care or, at worst, death |
| **Hazard log** | The list of hazards, controls and scores for a tool |
| **Hazard** | A situation in which a person, usually a patient, could come to harm. It always names a person. For example, a patient with red-flag symptoms not directed to urgent care in time |
| **IM1** (Interface Mechanism 1) | The NHS standard that lets other software connect to GP clinical systems. [Explainer](/explainers/im1) |
| **Incident log** | Your record of things that went wrong or nearly did, linked to the hazard log |
| **Large language model** (LLM) | The technology behind AI chatbots and most AI scribes, which writes by predicting likely words. [Explainer](/explainers/large-language-models) |
| **Lawful basis** | The legal reason for using personal information. For NHS care, usually public task, not consent |
| **LFPSE** | Learn from Patient Safety Events, the national incident recording service |
| **Medical device** | Software or equipment with a medical purpose, regulated by the MHRA. [Explainer](/explainers/medical-devices) |
| **National data opt-out** | Lets patients opt out of their information being used for research and planning; doesn’t apply to their care |
| **Personal data breach** | A security incident where personal information is lost, changed, or seen by someone who shouldn’t see it |
| **Post-deployment surveillance** | Checking a tool is still safe once it is in use |
| **Privacy notice** | What you publish to tell patients how their information is used |
| **Residual risk** | The risk left after controls are in place |
| **Mitigation** | Something that limits the harm if a hazard still happens, such as a same-day clinician review |
| **Risk** | How likely harm is, combined with how bad it would be |
| **Shadow AI** | AI used for work without the practice knowing or approving it, such as a free chatbot on a personal phone |
| **SIRO** (Senior Information Risk Owner) | The person accountable for information risk in the practice |
| **Special category data** | Types of personal information, including health, that the law protects more strongly |
| **Subject access request** | A request from a person for a copy of the information you hold about them |
| **Technology register** | A list of every digital tool the practice uses |
| **UK GDPR** | The main UK law on using personal information, alongside the Data Protection Act 2018 |
